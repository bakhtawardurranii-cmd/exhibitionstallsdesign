In-House or Outsourced Booth Fabrication: Who Should Build Your Stall?

When a builder says they build it themselves, what does that mean, and does it matter? A fair comparison, from a team that builds in-house.

In short: In-house fabrication usually gives more control, faster changes and one point of accountability. Outsourcing can work if it is well managed. What matters is knowing who builds what and who answers if something goes wrong. We do everything in-house, including printing.

At a glance

In-houseOutsourced
Quality controlDirect oversightDepends on supervision
Speed of changesUsually quickCan be slower
AccountabilityOne companyCan blur between companies
Hand-offsFewerMore
Peak-season capacityLimited by the workshopFlexible
Specialist skillsStrong in daily workPick a specialist per item

Where outsourcing works

Simple builds, one-off specialist items and busy seasons can all suit a well-managed outsourced model. The risk is in the hand-offs, where details get lost and responsibility blurs.

How to check what a builder really does

  • Ask which parts are made in their own workshop.
  • Ask for a video call and work-in-progress photos.
  • Ask who is accountable if a part fails or arrives late.
  • Check the quote names materials and who supplies them.

How we work

Design, 3D rendering, fabrication, graphics printing, installation and dismantling are all done by our own teams, with local workshops in Karachi, Islamabad and Lahore. Read about fabrication and printing.

Several cities, one standard: multi-city shows

If your calendar spans Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, ask each builder whether one team builds in every city or each city is passed to a local vendor. Quality tends to drift in the second case. We have our own workshops and teams in all three cities, with the head workshop in Karachi, so the same in-house process applies wherever the show is. See our workshop locations.

In-house CNC and fabrication vs outsourcing: cost and quality

  • Quality: the person who hears a change is the person who makes it.
  • Cost: fewer middle layers can mean fewer margins, though the quote scope matters more than the label. Compare using our guide to stall price in Pakistan.
  • Speed: a dimension change is a conversation, not an email chain.

A fair question for any builder: do you cut and assemble the boards in your own workshop, or buy finished parts and put them together? We cut our own: our workshops have CNC machines. Common questions

Is in-house always better?

Not always, but it usually gives more control and faster changes.

Does in-house mean everything is made by the builder?

Often not. Ask exactly which parts are made in the builder's own workshop. In our case, design, fabrication, printing, installation and dismantling are all in-house.

Is outsourcing cheaper?

It can be, but coordination and fixes can add cost. Compare what each quote includes.

How do I verify a workshop?

Ask for a video call and work-in-progress photos.

Does in-house fabrication change the cost and quality of a stall?

Usually it improves control and speed of changes. Cost depends on scope, so compare what each quote includes rather than the label.

Can in-house fabrication cover more than one city?

It can if the builder has workshops in each city. We have workshops and teams in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad.

Do you have CNC machines in-house?

Yes. Our workshops have CNC machines, so we cut stall parts ourselves instead of outsourcing them. That helps with speed of changes and with parts fitting the drawing.
