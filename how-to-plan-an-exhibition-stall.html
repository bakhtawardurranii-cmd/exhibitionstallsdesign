How Do You Plan an Exhibition Stall? Ten Steps in Order

Most stall problems are planning problems in disguise. Follow these ten steps in order and design, build and show day stop competing for the same few days.

In short: Plan an exhibition stall by setting one goal, reading the exhibitor manual, setting a budget, choosing a stall type, writing a brief, approving a 3D render, building, briefing your team, running the show and following up.

The ten steps

  1. Set one goal. Leads, launch, meetings or awareness. Make it measurable.
  2. Read the exhibitor manual. Note deadlines, build-up times and rules.
  3. Know your space. Stall number, size and how many sides are open.
  4. Set a budget. Include space, stall, graphics, installation, power, staff and a contingency.
  5. Choose a stall type. See stall types and starting prices.
  6. Write a brief. Goal, message, products, staff, logo files and budget.
  7. Approve a 3D render. Change things on screen, not in the workshop. See 3D rendering.
  8. Build and brand. Proofread graphics before printing.
  9. Brief your team and run the show. Rota for breaks, lead capture ready.
  10. Dismantle and follow up. Contact leads within days and compare results to your goal.

Mistakes worth avoiding

  • Designing before setting a goal
  • Trying to show every product in a small stall
  • Approving a design without checking it from the aisle
  • Sending print files at the last minute
  • Having no plan to follow up leads

Common questions

How early should I start planning?

As soon as you book your space. Design sign-off must reach us at least one week before the event, but larger stalls need far longer.

What is the first step?

Setting one clear goal for the show. Everything else follows from it.

How much should I budget?

Include space, the stall, graphics, installation, power, staff costs and a contingency. Our starting prices are on the stall types page.

Do I need a 3D render?

It is strongly recommended. It lets you check scale, flow and sightlines before anything is built.

Who should staff the stall?

Enough briefed people to cover breaks and capture leads without leaving the stall empty.
