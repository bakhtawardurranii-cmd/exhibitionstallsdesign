Lahore Booth Designer: Exhibition Booths That Survive Heat and Big Halls

Lahore summers punish materials and large halls punish weak sightlines. This page is a field guide to the physics of a stall, written for exhibitors who want to understand what they are paying for.

In short: As a Lahore booth designer we plan exhibition booth designing around summer heat and big-hall sightlines, and we fabricate in the warehouse in the basement of our Gulberg building.

Heat is a load, and a stall is part of the system

Lahore summers can push a hall and everything in it. Adhesives can creep and vinyl edges can lift, acrylic expands and needs room to move, fabric tension shifts with temperature, and LED power supplies need ventilation or they age early.

Dark finishes absorb more heat, and every extra screen adds electrical and thermal load. A well-planned stall leaves expansion gaps, ventilates electronics and budgets its power before the show, not during it.

Sightlines: how far away must your logo work?

In a big hall, most visitors first meet your stall from the end of an aisle, not from arm's length. Test your fascia at thumbnail size: if the name fails there, it fails on the floor. High contrast beats clever detail, and height beats width for being noticed over a crowd.

The three-distance test for a Lahore booth

Before a design leaves our screens we judge it from three places, because each one asks a different question.

  • Aisle end: can the name and one message be read? This decides whether anyone walks over.
  • Mid-aisle: does the counter, product or screen explain what you do? This decides whether they step in.
  • Arm's length: are the details, finishes and graphics worth touching? This decides whether they stay.

Common questions

How does Lahore's heat affect a stall?

Heat can loosen adhesives, expand panels and acrylic, change fabric tension and shorten the life of poorly ventilated LED drivers. We plan expansion gaps, ventilation and power load at design stage.

What lighting works best in a large hall?

Focused, even lighting on products and the brand wall usually outperforms bright but scattered light. Avoid glossy surfaces that reflect hall lighting into visitors' eyes.
