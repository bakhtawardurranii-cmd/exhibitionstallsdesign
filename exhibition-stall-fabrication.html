Exhibition Stall Fabrication in Pakistan: Custom, Modular and Shell Scheme Stalls

Fabrication is the stage where a drawing meets gravity. This page shows how a stall goes from frame to finish, and why building in-house, and test-assembling where needed, prevents most on-site problems.

In short: We are exhibition stall fabricators. We fabricate custom, modular and hybrid expo stalls and trade show booths in our own workshops in Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Faisalabad and Multan, using materials chosen for each job and test-assembling stalls before they ship where the project calls for it.

The build sequence

  1. Frame: timber or aluminium structure sized to the drawing.
  2. Panels: boards and sheets cut and fitted to the frame.
  3. Finish: paint, laminates or surface treatments, and edge sealing.
  4. Graphics and fittings: printed panels, lettering, lighting and electrics.
  5. Test assembly (where the project calls for it): the stall is built once in the workshop and checked.
  6. Label and pack: parts are numbered so on-site assembly follows the order they were tested.

Materials are chosen per job, not per habit

We work with the material that suits the requirement: MDF and plywood for shaped, paintable walls and counters, aluminium for light, reusable frames, acrylic for clean displays and illuminated surfaces, laminates for durable colour, fabric for seamless graphics, and LED for lighting.

The right choice depends on how often the stall is reused, how far it travels and your budget. Our exhibition stall materials guide compares each one, including wood fabrication for exhibition stands.

Custom, modular or hybrid?

A custom stall is built once to one design. A modular stall uses reusable frames and panels that can be reconfigured. A hybrid pairs a modular structure with custom elements such as a counter or feature wall. We build all three. See stall types and starting prices.

People search for this work in many ways: expo stall fabrication, trade show booth fabrication, exhibition stand fabrication, exhibition booth fabrication services. They all describe the same workshop job, and as exhibition stall manufacturers we do it end to end. We make:

  • The stall structure: the frame, walls and panels that define your space
  • The fascia: the name sign above the stall, often lit

From workshop to hall: our stall installation service

Fabrication ends on site. Our own crew of 5 to 10 people typically installs a stall in about three days and dismantles it after the show. The full sequence is on our stall installation and dismantling page, and our stall price list shows what the starting prices cover.

Where your stall is built

Rented stalls are mostly built on site inside the expo centre of each city. Custom products you buy outright are built in our workshops and, where your requirements call for it, test-assembled once before shipping so fit and finish are checked.

CNC cutting in our own workshops

Our workshops have CNC machines, so boards and panels for your stall are cut in-house and not bought in as finished parts. That suits curved portals, slatted walls, lettering and panel cut-outs, and it keeps parts matching the drawing, which makes test assembly and on-site fitting smoother. When a dimension changes, we change the file and cut again.

How long does stall fabrication take?

Fabrication time depends on the stall's size, design and finish. The easiest way to plan it is to work backwards from your show.

  1. Enquiry: we reply with a tailored quote within 24 hours.
  2. Design sign-off: needed at least one week before the event, and earlier for larger or custom stalls.
  3. Fabrication and printing: done in our workshop, with test assembly where the project calls for it.
  4. Installation: typically about three days on site with a crew of 5 to 10 people.
  5. Show opens: the stall is finished before the hall opens to visitors.

Send your show date with your enquiry and we confirm the timeline in writing. Use the printable exhibition booth checklist to track each stage, or read how to plan an exhibition stall in ten steps.

What to send a stall fabricator

Four items let a fabricator quote accurately and start sooner:

  • The exhibitor manual, which sets height, power and flooring rules.
  • Your stall number, size and position (inline, corner or island).
  • Your logo and a short brief: what you show and what visitors should remember.
  • The show name, hall and dates.

Already have drawings? We fabricate to them. If you need the design too, see exhibition stall design and stall designing.

CNC-cut or hand-cut stall parts: what changes for you

A CNC machine cuts parts from a digital file. A person still finishes and fits them. For you, that means:

  • Accuracy: every part matches the drawing, so panels fit when assembled.
  • Fast revisions: change the file and cut again, with no re-marking by hand.
  • Complex shapes: curved portals, slatted walls and lettering become practical.
  • Finishing: sanding, painting and sealing are still done by people, which is where quality shows.

Which board suits which job? Read exhibition stall materials compared.

Why in-house changes the outcome

Design, fabrication and printing happen inside our own teams, with local workshops in five cities. A change to a dimension becomes a conversation with the person building it, not an email chain. Test assembly means fit problems appear in the workshop, where they are cheap to fix.

What is the difference between a stall fabricator and a stall designer?

A designer plans the layout, branding and structure and produces 3D renders. A fabricator builds the stall from that drawing. We do both, so what you approve is what we make.

Do you offer expo stall fabrication in Lahore, Islamabad and Multan?

What are fabricated stalls?

Fabricated stalls are built to order in a workshop from boards, frames and panels, rather than pulled from a standard pre-built kit. They allow custom shapes, counters and branding, and are what we build alongside modular and shell stalls.
