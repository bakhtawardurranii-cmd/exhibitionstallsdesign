3D Booth Rendering: How to Check a Stall Before It Is Built

A render is a decision tool, not a picture. This page shows what it can reveal, what it cannot, and how to interrogate one so that the stall you approve is the stall you get.

In short: Our certified 3D artists produce renders that show scale, flow, lighting and graphics from several angles, using the same drawings our workshop builds from, so approved renders can actually be built.

What a render can tell you

  • Scale: whether the counter, shelves and walls feel right next to a human figure.
  • Flow: where visitors enter, pause and queue.
  • Sightlines: what the fascia looks like from the aisle end.
  • Light: how spotlights and backlit panels change mood.
  • Brand: logo size, colour and placement in context.

Seven questions to ask of any render

  1. Can the name be read from far away?
  2. Is there room to stand at the counter without blocking the entry?
  3. Which surfaces will face the main aisle?
  4. Where does the power and lighting come from?
  5. What is the floor finish?
  6. Are the materials shown the ones we will really use?
  7. What does the back and side view look like?

What a render cannot do

A render shows an idealised image. Real halls have glare, crowds and uneven light, and printed colours differ from screen colours. Treat renders as a guide to layout and proportion, and check critical colours against samples or references.

Why render and build under one roof

Some renders look stunning and are impossible to build. Ours are produced by certified 3D artists following structural engineering best practice, from the drawings our workshop builds from. Common questions

What is a 3D booth render?

A realistic computer image of your stall showing layout, materials, lighting and graphics from several angles before it is built.

How many revisions do I get?

We do not cap revisions. We work with you until you are satisfied, and most designs are finalised within a couple of revisions.

Will the finished stall match the render?

That is the aim of designing and building in-house. The approved render is the reference our workshop builds to.

Are the colours in a render exact?

Screen colours differ from printed colours, so we recommend checking critical brand colours against samples or references.

Do you render other event setups?

Yes. We render kiosks, counters, gates and event layouts too.
