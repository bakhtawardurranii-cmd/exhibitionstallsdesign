Multan Stall Designer: Exhibition Stalls That Spend Where Eyes Land

Small budgets can beat big ones if money goes to the right place. This page maps where attention falls on a stall, and what dry heat and dust mean for the materials you choose.

In short: As a Multan stall designer we do exhibition booth designing for regional shows, concentrating budget on the zones visitors notice and specifying surfaces that cope with dry heat and dust.

The attention map: where to put the money

Visitors scan a stall in seconds. Attention concentrates on the fascia, the eye-level band of the back wall, and the counter or product point. Spend there first. Hidden corners, high shelves and decorative extras rarely earn their cost, and a compact stall with three well-made zones outperforms a larger one spread thin.

Dry heat and dust: specify for them

Multan's hot, dry conditions favour sealed, washable surfaces over delicate ones. Dust dulls matte black and dark laminates quickly, so lighter or textured finishes forgive neglect. Display products under covers or in cases between visits, give LED drivers airflow, and expect adhesives to be tested by heat.

Five facts we ask a Multan exhibitor before we draw

  1. The size of the space and how many sides are open
  2. How many power points the hall provides, and where
  3. The floor finish, which decides how we fix and protect
  4. What one thing visitors should remember
  5. How many staff will be on the stall at once

Answers to these five usually save more money than any discount, because they stop us designing things you cannot use.

Common questions

Where should I spend a small stall budget?

On the fascia, the eye-level zone of the back wall and the counter or product display. These are the areas visitors notice first.

How does dust affect a stall?

Dust shows quickly on dark matte finishes. Lighter or textured finishes and covered displays keep a stall looking clean through a long event.

Do you have a Multan workshop?

We do not run a workshop in Multan. Our workshops are in Karachi, Islamabad and Lahore, and we design for and deliver to Multan exhibitions, with transport included in our starting prices.

Is a kiosk enough for a regional show?

Often, yes. A kiosk concentrates your message and costs less to run. It starts from PKR 15,000 as an installed rate.
